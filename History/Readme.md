## History of Motel Computers Ltd and their Cypher Z80/68000 SBC (1982-1986)

The following potted history is provided by the recollections of the author and from online public records. It is open to correction as need be.

Motel Computers Ltd of Willowdale (Toronto) Canada was incorporated as a Canadian federal company on May 12 1982. They began to fold up operation and liquidate their Cypher computer stock in the summer of 1986 and the corporation was dissolved on August 31 1989. However, Ian Cunningham was still providing CP/M-69k CBIOS updates as of June 1987.

The directors of the company were Victor Pierobin and David Hunter. Victor (born Valdagno, Italy, died May 2022) ran Victor Pierobon Consultants Ltd. at 174 Betty Ann Drive in Willowdale, Ontario, Canada. David was an engineer at Xerox (1982-1985) and later went on to be a medical physicist at Sunnybrook Research Institute (University of Toronto).

The software was developed by Ian A. Cunningham who had attained a PhD in Medical Biophsyics, University of Toronto, by 1986.

A bare bones kit was US$450 and an assembled basic kit was US\$1150 (Z80/68000, serial I/O, 128k DRAM, 4K static RAM and disk controller). A completely assembled kit was US\$1550 including 256k DRAM, 128k Video RAM, real time clock and both A/D and D/A. By August 1985 their pricing had been reduced to US\$400 for the bare board and US\$1200 for an assembled system with 1MB of DRAM. They also provided a hard disk controller plug-in board for US$150. The CPI from 1984 to today is roughly 2.7.

The curator of this repository purchased his assembled Cypher SBC system in July 1984 for CDN\$1500 (incl. tax) for the US$1150 basic kit version. It replaced his aging Exidy Sorcerer CP/M system and would be his primary hardware/software development computer until 1991 when he migrated to an IBM PC Pentium I. 

From the direct perspective of the curator, it was believed at the time that CP/M-based computers would (ideally) remain the dominant operating system well into the early to mid 1980s. The Cypher SBC arrived in the 1983-1984 era when CP/M-68k and 68000 CPUs were becoming more readily available. What the designers of the Cypher computer did not foresee was the rise and dominance of the IBM PC, DOS and 8086 processors which would have been a prime reason that the company disbanded the project in 1986. However, 68000 processors would still be popular with systems such as the Atari|ST, Amiga and Apple Macintosh (launched in 1984 and 1985).
